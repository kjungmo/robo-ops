\begin{abstract}
\sname\ is a lifelong fleet manager for grid warehouses: it allocates
pickup-and-delivery tasks, plans collision-free paths and manages batteries.
Assignment costs are arrival times at the pickup of the same windowed
space-time A* that plans the fleet, evaluated against busy robots'
reservations and fed to a Hungarian assignment. A hold cascade keeps
prioritized planning conflict-free, and endpoint capacities bound
dock queues. We prove that, under synchronous unit-time
execution and a replanning period no longer than the window, every executed
step is vertex- and swap-conflict-free; progress and
battery safety are not guaranteed, so we measure both. In simulation on
four synthetic layouts, an independent auditor finds no executed conflict in
4{,}740 runs with 60.4 million robot-steps; with normal batteries no robot
depletes, but with doubled drain the charging policy strands robots in two
thirds of runs. The coupling result is negative, weak and scoped to
allocation under a dock-capacity gate. Four
allocation cost models stay within 2.9\% in mean throughput in all twelve
cells, but the reservation-aware cost changes the matching in only 1--14\% of
the allocations. Three causes weaken the test: most pickups lie beyond the
planning window; under batch load the dock gate leaves one candidate task in
most calls with 32 robots; event-triggered epochs offer one idle robot at a
time. Only 9--26\% of the calls are real
assignment problems. With event epochs off (chosen after a five-seed hint), 30
seeds in two 32-robot cells detect no difference ($+0.4\pm1.1\%$ and
$+0.6\pm1.5\%$ for \sname\ over Hungarian). The study omits full-trip and dock-queue costs. With one robot per dock, token passing with task swaps
and an interruptible drive home beats our loop by 0.9--3.7\%. Dock capacity,
the planning window and, on a small layout, windowed conflict-based search move
the fleet. Code: \url{https://github.com/kjungmo/robo-ops}
\end{abstract}
