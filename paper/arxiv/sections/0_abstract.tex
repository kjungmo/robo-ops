\begin{abstract}
Fleet managers for warehouse mobile robots must allocate a stream of
pickup-and-delivery tasks, plan collision-free paths for every robot and keep
batteries from running out, all in a loop that never stops. The three problems
are usually solved by separate modules connected through free-space distance
estimates, and the literature on coupling them tightly consists of one-shot
solvers that do not fit a lifelong setting. In this paper we introduce
\emph{\sname}, a complete lifelong fleet manager for grid warehouses that
couples the modules the cheap way: assignment costs are arrival times of the
same windowed space-time A* that plans the fleet, evaluated against the live
reservation table, and fed to an optimal Hungarian assignment, while a hold
cascade keeps prioritized planning conflict-free by construction and
token-passing endpoint capacities keep dock queues resolvable. We prove
conflict-freeness of each replanning epoch and optimality of the assignment
step, and evaluate the whole stack in a seeded discrete-time simulator with
Poisson task streams, loading dwell and an explicit battery model on three
layouts, one of them derived from an operator console's own zone and dock
data. Across 420 runs and 8.8 million robot-steps the executed plans contain
zero conflicts, no robot depletes and no run deadlocks. The controlled study
of the coupling is a negative result that we report as such: greedy,
Hungarian, congestion-proxy and MAPF-in-the-loop costs stay within 2.9\% of
each other in throughput in every one of twelve layout/fleet/load cells,
whereas dock capacity ($+26$--$29\%$), the planning window ($\pm7\%$) and an
optional windowed CBS backend ($-8\%$ makespan on small fleets) are the levers
that move the fleet. Code, results and this paper are at
\url{https://github.com/kjungmo/robo-ops}.
\end{abstract}
