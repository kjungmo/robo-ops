\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:concl}

We presented \sname, a lifelong fleet manager for grid warehouses that runs
task allocation, windowed prioritized path planning and charging in one
rolling-horizon loop, couples allocation to planning through arrival-time
costs of the same planner, and keeps every epoch conflict-free by a hold
cascade whose conflict-freeness we proved. An independent trajectory auditor
found no executed conflict in 4{,}740 seeded runs with 60.4 million
robot-steps across four layouts, fleets of 4 to 48 robots, two load
regimes and both planner backends; removing the cascade brings conflicts
back and removing the dynamic priorities gridlocks the fleet. With normal
batteries no robot depleted and every run delivered all of its tasks; with
doubled drain the threshold charging policy stranded robots whenever the
slots saturated, which the conditional reserve bound we proved explains but
does not prevent. The coupled allocation did not improve throughput or
service time over static Hungarian or even greedy assignment, neither in the
twelve cells of the main study nor in four regimes chosen beforehand to
favour it, while dock capacity and the planning window changed throughput
by 7--29\%, for small fleets a windowed CBS backend shortened the makespan
by 8\%, and token passing lost 16--66\% to its endpoint rule.
The implementation, the benchmark and every number in this paper are
available in the operator console repository so that the study can be
repeated on layouts where the balance may differ.
