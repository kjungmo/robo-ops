\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\noindent\textbf{Multi-agent path finding.}
Optimal MAPF on graphs is NP-hard \citep{yu2013structure}; search-based solvers
are surveyed by \citet{felner2017search} and benchmarked under the common
definitions of \citet{stern2019mapf}. Conflict-Based Search (CBS)
\citep{sharon2015cbs} and its improvements
\citep{boyarski2015icbs,li2019symmetry,li2021eecbs} are optimal or
bounded-suboptimal; prioritized planning
\citep{erdmann1987multiple,vandenberg2005prioritized,cap2015prioritized} with a
space-time reservation table \citep{silver2005cooperative} is incomplete but
fast and widely used. PIBT
\citep{okumura2019pibt,okumura2022pibt} replaces long-horizon paths with
one-step priority inheritance, and in concurrent work
\citet{zheng2026learning} learn the priority order of rolling-horizon
prioritized planning for lifelong warehouses. \sname\ uses windowed
prioritized planning with a fixed, hand-written dynamic priority rule as its
default planner (\cref{subsec:planning}) and offers windowed CBS as an
optional backend.

\noindent\textbf{Lifelong pickup-and-delivery.}
Warehouse fleets never solve a one-shot instance: tasks arrive continuously
\citep{wurman2008coordinating}. \citet{ma2017lifelong} formalised
multi-agent pickup and delivery (MAPD), defined well-formed MAPD instances
(building on the well-formed infrastructures of \citealp{cap2015complete}) and
the token-passing (TP) scheme, in which a robot only takes a task whose pickup
and delivery are not the endpoint of another robot's path; its variant with
task swaps (TPTS) lets a robot take over an assigned task whose pickup it
reaches earlier than the current assignee, which already couples allocation
to planned arrival times, greedily and one robot at a time.
\citet{ma2019lifelong} added kinematic constraints
and \citet{liu2019task} studied task sequencing. RHCR \citep{li2021rhcr}
showed that replanning every $h$ steps over a window of $w$ steps scales to
hundreds of robots. RHCR is a framework: its authors note that regular MAPF
solvers can be adapted to its windowed subproblem. Our rolling-horizon loop with static Hungarian costs and windowed
prioritized planning is an instance of it with task assignment and charging
added, so we do not implement RHCR a second time, and we ablate the one
place where our planner differs from plain windowed prioritized planning,
the handling of planner failures (\cref{subsec:cascade}). The concurrent
LSMART testbed \citep{yan2026lsmart} studies when to plan, how to plan and
how to recover from planner failures in lifelong fleet management systems
with realistic execution, using a random goal assigner. \sname\ adopts
the RHCR execution model and TP-style endpoint reservation, varies the
assignment cost instead, and compares against TP and TPTS re-implemented in
the same simulator (\cref{subsec:baselines}).

\noindent\textbf{Planning failures, batteries and docks.}
A windowed planner can fail to return a valid plan, and a lifelong system
needs a rule for what the robots do then. \citet{morag2023adapting}
formalise such fail policies: under IStay the agents whose paths are invalid
stay in place, which can invalidate further paths whose agents then also
stay, and IAvoid additionally lets them make one avoiding move. They also
describe two ways for prioritized planning to return a partial plan:
\emph{Restart}, which starts again with a new priority order, and
\emph{Persist}, which skips an agent without a path and continues with the
next. LSMART
supports making all robots wait in place and compares re-planning with PIBT,
guided PIBT and a local repair that inserts waits \citep{yan2026lsmart}. Our
hold cascade
(\cref{alg:pp}) is a variant of these policies for prioritized planning: as
under Persist, planning continues past a robot without a path; the
failed robot holds its cell, and the robots whose reservations cross that
cell are removed and re-planned against the hold instead of being made to
stay; held robots get high priority in the next epoch. The priority-restart
arm of our ablation (\cref{subsec:cascade}) is a restart scheme with a
deterministic promotion of the failed robot instead of a new random order. Energy has been added
to MAPD by \citet{kudo2024anytime}, who study multi-task MAPD under power
limits and charge speeds, and by EL-MAPD \citep{tang2026energy}, which defines
well-formed energy-limited lifelong instances and a fallback priority
planning that obtains a feasible action in every planning episode. The
charging policy of \sname\ is a threshold heuristic without such a
guarantee; we state a conditional bound and measure where the policy fails
(\cref{subsec:charging,subsec:main}). Capacity at the endpoints matters as
well: \citet{kou2020idle} assign robots to sortation stations by min-cost
flow so as to minimise station idle time, and, in concurrent work, HARR
coordinates handover stations that have single docks and finite buffers
through a dock reservation calendar \citep{zang2026handover}. \sname\ bounds
the number of robots in flight per dock with a capacity version of the
token-passing endpoint rule and
measures the effect of that bound (\cref{subsec:ablation}).

\noindent\textbf{Task allocation and its coupling with path finding.}
Assignment problems are classically solved by the Hungarian method
\citep{kuhn1955hungarian,munkres1957algorithms,jonker1987shortest} or by
auctions \citep{bertsekas1988auction}; taxonomies of multi-robot task
allocation are given by \citet{gerkey2004formal} and
\citet{korsah2013comprehensive}. Coupling assignment with MAPF has been done
optimally for teams of interchangeable agents (CBM, \citealp{ma2016optimal})
and for agents with individual sets of admissible goals (CBS-TA,
\citealp{honig2018cbsta}), at a cost that has kept these methods to one-shot
instances; \citet{fan2025joint} extend a CBS-based joint task allocation and
path finding search to continuous pickup and delivery by updating its task
allocation as tasks arrive. Approximate coupling in online MAPD is also
established. Besides TPTS, \citet{shimokawa2022improvement} estimate pickup
times to choose the robot for a new task in token passing, and
\citet{cilden2025task} evaluate task-assignment strategies for capacitated
agents in lifelong pickup and delivery. RMCA evaluates
assignments of task sequences with collision-free paths from prioritized
planning, improves them by large neighbourhood search, and re-runs on the
released tasks at every timestep \citep{chen2021integrated}. LNS-wPBS builds
assignments by Hungarian-based insertion on service-time estimates that
ignore collisions, improves them by large neighbourhood search and plans
with windowed PBS; in its comparison, the search started from
Hungarian-based insertion gives the smallest service time in most cases,
which the authors read as Hungarian-based insertion being a better initial
assignment than a greedy heuristic
\citep{xu2022multigoal}. In concurrent work, Detour-Delta ranks candidate
tasks by the extra delivery time that committed space-time reservations
impose beyond the geometric shortest path, and reports gains on layouts with
single-agent aisles, dead ends and tree-like structure, as well as gains
over its base planner on well-formed maps \citep{hirayama2026detour}.
Flow-based assignment with congestion-aware edge costs \citep{zhang2025flow}
and GRAND, which combines learned guidance with rebalancing and assignment
\citep{gaber2026grand}, report gains in evaluations that include fleets far
larger than ours; \citet{chen2024traffic} use traffic-flow estimates to
guide the paths, not the assignments, of lifelong MAPF. Outside grid MAPD, \citet{forte2021online} assign tasks
online to fleets of coordinated robots and count the interference between
robots as a cost of the assignment. \citet{zhu2026combined} study online assignment
together with lifelong path finding on data from a deployed warehouse, where
the assigner chooses a robot's delivery port, and conclude that the
assignment module, like the routing module, should exploit the layout.
\sname's coupling is a cheap member of this family: assignment costs are
arrival times at the pickup of windowed space-time A* against the
reservations of the robots that are already busy, solved jointly for all
idle robots by the Hungarian method. It prices the traffic that already
exists but not the loaded leg, the dock queue or the interactions among the
robots being assigned. None of these ingredients is new on its own; the
contribution is their combination with batteries and dock capacities in one
loop, and the controlled measurement of what this particular coupling buys
there.
